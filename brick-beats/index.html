Example mat
    Tips for a good photo, and limits

    Tips

    • Hold the phone straight above the mat, the whole mat in view with floor showing on all four sides.
    • Soft, even light: no glare spots or hard shadows (a shadow of your hand or phone counts). Avoid the flash.
    • A plain floor behind the mat works best; busy rugs, stripes and dark floors are harder.
    • Keep the tape lanes straight and about the same width and distance apart.
    • Hold still and let the camera focus. Blurry photos hide small bricks.
    • Reading is not right? Use Fix corners, then Adjust grid (sliders), then Re-read notes. Tap a brick to fix its colour.

    Limits

    • The whole mat must be in the photo; a mat cut off by the frame cannot be straightened by itself.
    • Bricks of nearly the same colour (pale yellow, white, light lime) can be mixed up.
    • Colours that match the mat or the tape (green or blue bricks on a green mat, blue tape) are easy to miss.
    • Lego studs are half the size of Duplo ones: the app reads whatever stud size it finds, so lengths may come out double. Fix with Adjust grid.
    • Everything is read on your phone: no photo leaves it, and there is no internet or AI involved.

    Reading the mat…

    Try an example:

    Adjust grid. Even spacing (default): lanes and columns are evenly spaced, so set where they start, how big they are and the gap (tape) with the sliders on the left and below the photo. Switch it off to drag single lane lines or the column edges. Letting go of a slider re-reads the notes; Re-read notes does it on demand and drops your hand edits.

    Lanes

    How it works

    • Lane = a playerEach strip between the tape plays its own instrument.
    • Colour = the noteTap a colour below to hear it.
    • Long brick = long noteCount the studs along the brick.
    • Left to right = timeThe orange line shows where the music is.
