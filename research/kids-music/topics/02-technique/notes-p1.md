# 02 Technique — P1 notes

## Topline
- Setup first: feet supported (footrest), forearms roughly level, elbows near key height, relaxed shoulders; a too-high/low bench causes collapsed or raised wrists. A game cannot enforce this, but can prompt a one-time "set-up check".
- Piano Safari (Hague dissertation) teaches non legato first, then legato, then staccato, to build hand shape, relaxed forearm and aim. Games should start with detached, arm-driven notes, not "smooth" finger play.
- Flat fingers in kids often come from reaching, cognitive load or bench setup, not laziness. Fix the cause (move the hand sooner, reduce load); don't nag one finger. Games should not punish flat fingers.
- Ukulele is the common starter for ages 5-6; guitar from about 7 (teacher-site consensus, not studies). Realistic sessions: 5-10 min at age 4-5, 10-15 at 6-7, 15-20 at 8-9. Fretted chords are not realistic under about 6.
- Interleaved practice helps retention in some music studies, but results are mixed and learners prefer blocked practice because it feels fluent. Games can interleave "for free"; short evidence for young beginners is thin.
- Motivation: research on 157 children (via Evans 2015 SDT review) found no child whose parents used rewards for practice continued past a year. Autonomy, competence and relatedness are the lever; routines (same time, daily 5-10 min) and progress feel-good matter.
- Steady beat is a taught skill, felt through touch/movement before it is heard; Feierabend suggests short recordings at about 120-136 beats/min for movement. Metronome work for 3-5s should be game-like, slow, clapping/marching.
- Tension and finger-only playing are the main injury risks; instruments sized wrong and soreness from long sessions matter. Games should cap session length and reward rest/reset.

## Cards

### Q1 Posture and hand shape

### Piano posture for beginners (Tonestro)
- URL: https://www.tonestro.com/blog/piano/piano-posture-and-hand-position-for-beginners
- Source: Tonestro, 2026, vendor blog (piano app) / practitioner guidance
- Subq: Q1, Q7
- Claims:
  - [practice] Sit on the front half of the bench, feet supported, forearms about level with the keyboard, elbows near key height.
  - [practice] Too-low bench: wrists bend up and shoulders rise. Too-high: wrists collapse and fingers feel crowded.
  - [practice] A child whose feet don't reach the floor needs a stable footrest; a firm cushion is a temporary height fix, a soft one is not.
  - [practice] Wrist should be neutral but mobile, not locked; move the whole hand/forearm to a new area rather than stretching fingers.
  - [practice] Don't force every finger into the same curve; finger lengths differ. Keep fingertips near keys; smaller movements are easier to control.
  - [practice] If shoulders rise on hard passages, slow down and simplify instead.
- For our games: a "set-up check" screen (feet, height, distance) before play; difficulty ramps should be slow enough that kids don't tense up.
- Caveats: vendor blog, written for adults and children together; no study cited.

### Why kids play with flat fingers (iPianoLab)
- URL: https://ipianolab.com/blog/child-plays-piano-with-flat-fingers
- Source: iPianoLab, undated, blog (teacher-style guidance)
- Subq: Q1, Q7
- Claims:
  - [fact] Flat fingers are common in young beginners and rarely have one cause.
  - [practice] Causes: reaching for the next note, concentrating on reading/counting (cognitive load), poor bench/keyboard setup, and weak ring/little finger independence.
  - [practice] Fix by moving the whole hand sooner or simplifying the task, not by repeatedly telling one finger to curve.
  - [practice] Observe when the shape changes (easy vs new music, one hand vs two) and report that to the teacher.
- For our games: don't gate success on a camera-judged finger curve; when kids fail, shrink the task (fewer notes, no reading) before judging shape.
- Caveats: blog; no data; mixes parent advice with teacher referral.

### Piano posture (Teach Piano Today, search snippet only)
- URL: https://www.teachpianotoday.com/piano-posture-and-hand-position
- Source: Teach Piano Today, undated, teacher blog
- Subq: Q1
- Claims:
  - [practice] Elbows should sit at roughly 90 degrees with fingers on keys; if bent too much, move the bench back.
- For our games: simple, checkable cue ("elbows about square") that parents can verify.
- Caveats: only snippet read; not extracted.

### Q2 Early piano progressions

### Foundations of Piano Technique (Piano Safari)
- URL: https://www.pianosafari.com/field-notes/foundations-of-piano-technique
- Source: Piano Safari (Julie Hague, Katherine Fisher), method publisher, drawing on Hague's dissertation
- Subq: Q1, Q2, Q7
- Claims:
  - [fact] Dissertation finding as reported: teach non legato (portato) first, then legato, then staccato.
  - [practice] Extended non legato early prevents common technical problems and builds a relaxed forearm, singing tone and good hand shape; also builds aim as the arm aligns the finger with the key.
  - [practice] Repeated-note exercise with a rest between each finger lets the child reset hand shape if it slips.
  - [practice] Legato is introduced as arm-weight transfer with a small arm "bounce", keeping the arm involved and relaxed.
  - [practice] Wrist-lift off long notes (the "giraffe" gesture) teaches release.
  - [opinion] Animal imagery makes technique memorable for children.
- Quote: "best to teach non legato (portato) articulation first"
- For our games: first note-playing tasks should reward clear, detached, arm-driven notes with a built-in reset beat; animal/gesture metaphors for technique. Games should not start with "play smoothly".
- Caveats: method publisher (vendor); dissertation not read directly; one method among several.

### Five-finger warm-ups (Shirley Kirsten blog) and Pianote beginner exercises
- URL: https://arioso7.wordpress.com/2011/01/12/piano-instruction-five-finger-warm-ups-in-major-and-minor-video ; https://www.pianote.com/blog/beginner-exercises
- Source: Arioso7 blog (experienced teacher, 2011); Pianote (vendor, online lessons)
- Subq: Q2
- Claims:
  - [practice] Teach five-finger positions as a pattern/shape (a grouping), not note by note; practise both legato and staccato versions.
  - [practice] Most students can start major/minor five-finger positions around the 6th-9th month of lessons; younger children learning notation may need longer, and Faber Primer uses a floating-note five-finger warm-up early.
  - [practice] Start hands separately, then together; control and even pressure before speed.
  - [practice] Contrast exercise: legato in one hand, staccato in the other, then swap (hand independence).
- For our games: five-finger "shape" games on a fixed position (C or G) with note names optional; contrast mode (smooth vs bouncy) as a later unlock; speed only after evenness.
- Caveats: snippets only; no age data for 4-10; Pianote is vendor.

### Q3 Guitar and ukulele

### Teach Ukulele to Kids (ukulelehistory.info)
- URL: https://ukulelehistory.info/teach-ukulele-kids-guide
- Source: ukulelehistory.info, undated, educator blog
- Subq: Q3, Q4, Q5
- Claims:
  - [practice] Age 4-5: 5-10 min a couple of times a week; hold instrument, strum open strings, sing, keep a beat; fretted chords not realistic yet.
  - [practice] Age 6-7: 10-15 min, three times a week; one-finger chords (C, A minor) and simple two-chord songs.
  - [practice] Age 8-9: 15-20 min most days; three or four chords, real strumming pattern, playing with others; no barre chords.
  - [practice] Under 8s struggle to press one string without muting the next; the one-finger C chord is the entry point.
  - [practice] Doane method order: C, F, G7, G; each new chord unlocks 10+ songs.
  - [practice] Correct one thing per session and praise the rest; repeated "that's wrong" makes kids quit.
  - [fact] Sore fingertips last about two weeks; calluses form in 10-14 days of daily practice; new nylon strings drift out of tune for the first week.
  - [practice] Two tables on the page disagree slightly (e.g. "5-6: 10 min twice a day" vs "4-5: twice a week"), so treat timings as indicative.
- For our games: ukulele-first path; strum-and-sing before chords; one-finger chord level; unlock songs per chord; limit sessions to 5-15 min; one correction at a time.
- Caveats: blog with internally inconsistent tables; not a study; may be SEO content.

### Ukulele vs guitar start ages (44School, TuneLark)
- URL: https://www.44school.com/guitar-lessons.html ; https://www.tunelark.com/blog/ukulele-or-guitar-for-beginners
- Source: 44School (music school, vendor); TuneLark (vendor blog)
- Subq: Q3
- Claims:
  - [opinion] Seven is usually the earliest sensible age for guitar; start at five or six on ukulele or piano and move across around seven.
  - [opinion] Even a half-size guitar is hard for a five-year-old (size, shape, six strings vs four).
  - [practice] Under 7: ukulele; guitar from 7 to 9.
- For our games: don't target fretted guitar below 7; ukulele content for 5-7.
- Caveats: lesson businesses; consensus of practice, not evidence. Snippets only.

### Ukulele Magazine first lesson
- URL: https://ukulelemagazine.com/lessons/your-first-ukulele-lesson-a-beginners-guide-to-playing-ukulele
- Source: Ukulele Magazine, magazine lesson
- Subq: Q3
- Claims:
  - [practice] Foundation is steady, rhythmic down-strokes counted 1-2-3-4 while singing a familiar song.
  - [practice] Check thumb and wrist relaxation; fingertip placement; clean ringing needs fingers on fingertips.
  - [practice] Move between chords by finding economical finger paths (anchor fingers).
- For our games: count-in and steady down-strum game with singing; chord-change "anchor finger" puzzles.
- Caveats: adult beginners; snippet only.

### Q4 Practice science

### Blocked vs interleaved practice (Frontiers / PMC study)
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4989027
- Source: PMC (journal article), advanced players, 10 participants in the main study; reviews earlier child studies
- Subq: Q4
- Claims:
  - [fact] Stambaugh (2009): fifth/sixth-grade beginner clarinetists practising three 7-note patterns interleaved played faster at retention than blocked.
  - [fact] Other studies (seventh-grade wind players) found no schedule effect on accuracy, so results are mixed.
  - [fact] Most participants found interleaving more useful but still preferred blocked, because repetition feels fluent.
  - [opinion] Interleaving lowers felt fluency, so learners may underestimate what they retain.
- Quote: "providing a (misleading) impression of learning" (Kornell and Bjork, 2008)
- For our games: mix 2-3 targets inside a short round; show progress across days rather than within a round; expect kids to feel "worse" in interleaved rounds.
- Caveats: advanced players (small sample); child findings summarised secondhand.

### Spaced practice for piano (Learning Scientists) and Yamaha practice types
- URL: https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/11/09-1 ; https://hub.yamaha.com/music-educators/prof-dev/teaching-tips/types-of-practice
- Source: Learning Scientists (summary of Wiseheart et al. study); Yamaha Music (vendor teacher hub, citing Barbara Fast)
- Subq: Q4
- Claims:
  - [fact] Wiseheart et al. found spacing of 1-15 minutes made no difference to a piano learning task; the blogger notes spacing intervals were short.
  - [opinion] Yamaha hub argues blocked practice feels good but interleaved gives better long-term retention, and varied practice raises engagement.
  - [practice] Alternating tasks (ABCBACACB) is the model; naive repetition is called ineffective.
- For our games: spaced return (revisit yesterday's pattern) is plausible; don't promise short-gap spacing helps.
- Caveats: Yamaha is vendor; spacing effect untested at day scale for piano here.

### Q5 Motivation

### Self-determination theory in music education (Evans, 2015)
- URL: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2015_Evans_SDT_MusicEdu.pdf
- Source: Paul Evans, 2015, review (SDT website, peer-reviewed article)
- Subq: Q5
- Claims:
  - [fact] Faulkner, Davidson and McPherson (2010), 157 children: some parents paid for practice with allowance or TV time; no child in that group continued past one year or played as an adult.
  - [number] 157 children in the cited study.
  - [fact] Parent involvement correlates modestly with achievement and most strongly with motivation outcomes (a meta-type finding of 406 children).
  - [opinion] External rewards are intuitively tempting but can undermine internalised motivation; SDT stresses autonomy, competence and relatedness.
- For our games: avoid pay-for-practice mechanics (points for minutes); reward mastery of a skill, give choice of song/instrument, and include shared play with a parent.
- Caveats: correlational; snippet-level read; ages vary.

### Motivation in learning instruments (Foldi et al., 2024) and Allegro Music
- URL: https://publicatio.bibl.u-szeged.hu/36481/1/foldi-et-al-2024-motivation-in-learning-musical-instruments-the-role-of-parents-teachers-and-friends.pdf ; https://www.allegromusicpublishing.com/post/the-art-of-encouragement-extrinsic-motivation-in-music-education-for-children
- Source: Foldi et al., 2024, study (Hungary); Allegro Music Publishing, vendor blog
- Subq: Q5
- Claims:
  - [fact] For the youngest learners parents are the main influence; for older teens friends' feedback dominates.
  - [fact] Students who quit instruments showed low autonomy, competence and relatedness.
  - [practice] Identified regulation ("practice helps me improve") is a healthier extrinsic form than guilt-driven.
  - [opinion] Allegro: sticker charts and rewards work as occasional boosts, not constant crutches.
- For our games: design for parent-and-child co-play in 4-7; peer features later; connect rewards to goals the child understands.
- Caveats: snippets only; ages mostly teens; vendor blog.

### Parent motivation tips (Davidson Institute) and Suzuki triangle (SAA)
- URL: https://www.davidsongifted.org/gifted-blog/tips-for-parents-motivation-what-really-works-with-kids-and-music-practice ; https://suzukiassociation.org/2023/10/31/learning-from-every-corner-of-the-suzuki-triangle
- Source: Davidson Institute (talk summary by music educator Barbie Wong); Suzuki Association of the Americas (teacher essay, 2023)
- Subq: Q5
- Claims:
  - [practice] Most kids dislike practice because it is hard; that is normal. Kids copy, so they need to hear, see and make music with others.
  - [practice] Progress drives motivation; practise daily even 5-10 minutes to build the habit; same time and place daily.
  - [practice] Suzuki triangle: teacher, parent and student share lessons, home practice and the musical environment; parents carry much of the pressure.
  - [practice] Suzuki line: practise "only on the days you eat" removes negotiation.
  - [fact] Daily progress is messy ("two steps forward, one back"), unlike the smoother weekly view a teacher sees.
- For our games: show a parent-facing view that normalises dips; fixed daily slot reminder; short daily goal; co-play mode.
- Caveats: a summary of a talk and a personal essay; no data.

### Q6 Ear, singing, steady beat

### Steady beat (Feierabend Association, Connie Greenwood)
- URL: https://www.feierabendmusic.org/steady-beat-connie-greenwood
- Source: Feierabend Association for Music Education, practitioner article
- Subq: Q6
- Claims:
  - [practice] Children must first experience steady beat through touch (tapping on legs/body parts); repetition and joyful singing/dancing build it through elementary years.
  - [practice] Around 1.5-2 years kids imitate beat-keeping; adults should follow the child's own inner beat before imposing one.
  - [number] Feierabend: move to recordings no longer than 2-3 minutes, at about 120-136 taps/min (about 2 taps/sec).
  - [opinion] A cited claim (Weikert) that steady-beat independence links to reading, maths and behaviour; strong claim, unverified here.
  - [fact] Adults and teachers in a 15-country survey (about 2,000 teachers, 5,000 parents) rated beat as of moderate importance and thought it needed no teaching.
- For our games: beat games should use body-tap input and short songs at about 2 beats/sec; tolerate child tempo first, then add a fixed pulse.
- Caveats: association advocacy; Weikert claim not independently checked.

### Metronome with kids (Parramatta music school) and pitch-matching development
- URL: https://musiclessonsparramatta.com.au/metronome-practice-teach-kids-steady-rhythm-skills-today ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13252975
- Source: Music school blog (vendor); PMC article (Welch's four phases cited secondhand)
- Subq: Q6
- Claims:
  - [practice] Ages 3-5: metronome practice as a game, clapping/marching at slow beats, aim at awareness not perfection; 60-80 BPM, 5-10 minutes.
  - [practice] Kids rush when excited; start very slow and speed up only after consistency.
  - [practice] Ages 6-8: 70-100 BPM, 10-15 min; some children try to make the metronome follow them.
  - [fact] Welch: pitch-matching develops in four phases, from word-biased narrow-range singing to few pitch errors on simple songs; Davidson proposed completion by about age 8.
  - [practice] Kodály approach: movable-do solfa with hand signs and movement, built for children.
  - [practice] Children's voices are strongest around F-G in mid range; prefer head voice, avoid belting.
- For our games: slow default tempos (60-100 BPM), pitch games in a mid, narrow range, expect pitch accuracy to keep developing to about 8.
- Caveats: BPM table from a vendor with no sources; PMC article is about hearing-device users; Kodály snippets from Musical U and a choir page.

### Q7 Injury and bad habits

### Healthy hands and injury prevention (Vibe Music Academy; PianoCareer)
- URL: https://www.vibemusicacademy.com/blog/2026/2/12/healthy-hands-injury-prevention-for-young-musicians ; https://www.pianocareer.com/piano-health/avoid-piano-injuries
- Source: Vibe Music Academy (studio blog, 2026); PianoCareer (teacher blog)
- Subq: Q7, Q1
- Claims:
  - [practice] Growing bodies are more vulnerable to tension; wrong-size setups need correcting with the teacher.
  - [practice] Beginners often play with "claw hands"; wrists don't need to be ramrod straight; hunching is a common fault.
  - [practice] Playing "only with fingers" without arm involvement leads to tension and pain; tension is the main risk factor.
  - [practice] Posture is a fluid process, not a static hold; sitting too close with elbows glued to sides causes bent wrists.
  - [opinion] PianoCareer argues against five-finger legato in the early beginner stage and old "finger" drill books; one teacher's view.
  - [practice] Reduce tempo and difficulty when tension appears.
- For our games: forced finger-drill games (rapid repeated notes) risk tension; include rest beats, caps on repetitions and a "shake it out" break.
- Caveats: blogs, no clinical evidence; PianoCareer is one teacher's opinion; no child-specific incidence data.

## Gaps
- No peer-reviewed studies on piano/guitar technique for ages 4-10 (hand shape, bench height, injury incidence).
- Slow practice and chunking/motor learning for young children: not covered by any card.
- Guitar for kids beyond ukulele (fretting-hand shape, nylon vs steel, string action): only vendor snippets.
- Looking at hands vs reading ahead: no source found.
- Rhythm/tempo drift ("rushing") evidence for children and how games should score timing.
- Practice-time norms by age beyond vendor tables; Suzuki Association primary guidance on practice length for 4-7.
- Rewards research for young children specifically (most SDT music studies involve older kids/teens).
