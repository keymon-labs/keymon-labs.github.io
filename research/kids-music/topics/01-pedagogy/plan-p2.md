# 01 Pedagogy — P2 plan

Budget for the P2 gatherer: 10 searches, 10 extracts. The allocation per question is below; the total is 9 searches and 10 extracts, which leaves 1 spare search.

## What we know (judged)
- **Solid:** sound before symbol is the consensus across Kodály, Orff, Gordon, Suzuki and Feierabend. Several independent practitioner sources agree on it. It is still practitioner consensus and not trial evidence (no controlled study was found).
- **Solid on the sequence, weak on the reason:** the Kodály pitch ladder is so-mi, then la, then do-re, with fa and ti last. The OAKE association source confirms the pentatonic start. The reason given (half steps are hard to sing, so-mi is a "natural" playground call) comes only from Wikipedia and blog snippets and needs checking.
- **Solid as practice:** steady beat comes before rhythm, and rhythm is introduced through word rhythms and then named (ta/ti-ti). This matches Musical Development Matters (MDM, a UK framework) and 04's finding that beat synchronisation matures between ages 5 and 7–10. The "a full year on beat" figure comes from a vendor blog.
- **Solid for under-6s only:** the MDM PDF (primary framework) covers pitch matching, contour, inner singing and pulse at 40–60 months, and says the bands overlap. **We have no sourced ladder for ages 6–10.** This is the biggest hole, because most of the games target 6–10.
- **Real disagreement, not settled:** movable do versus fixed do, and Kodály versus Gordon rhythm syllables. Both rest on opinion blogs. Our users in Spain and the rest of Europe will expect fixed do, but we have no source on how Spanish schools or conservatorios actually teach it.
- **Plausible, single source:** the child singing range D4–A4 for under-5s (ChoralNet, a secondary summary) and the "high means loud" confusion (one teacher blog). Both directly shape game parameters.
- **Thin, upper-elementary only:** harmony enters through a drone/bordun, then I–IV–V roots, then chord tones (one conference handout). We have nothing on scales, keys, major/minor or piano accompaniment patterns.
- **Weak:** Orff's four stages (imitate, explore, improvise, compose) and the "fill 8 beats" scaffold come from snippets only. They are plausible and widely repeated.

## Phase 2 questions (ranked)

### 1. What is the skill ladder for ages 6–10 (grades 1–4)?
- Why it matters for the games: level unlock order across all games (which rhythm values, which pitches, when to read, when to improvise). Without it, the level design for 6–10 is guesswork.
- Queries: `Kodaly concept sequence grade 1-4`; `elementary music curriculum sequence rhythm melody`
- Sources: Kodály/OAKE curriculum documents, university theses, open textbooks.
- Extract (pick 3, in this order): https://atumusic4kids.files.wordpress.com/2016/11/kodalysequencefinal.pdf ; https://milnepublishing.geneseo.edu/music-and-the-child/chapter/chapter-8 ; https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc146441/m2/1/high_res_d/1995-23_Navarro.pdf
- Output wanted: a table by grade/age covering rhythm elements, pitch set, reading, inner hearing, part-work, improvisation.
- Budget: 1–2 searches, 3 extracts.

### 2. Fixed do or movable do for our players, and how does Spain teach note names?
- Why it matters for the games: note labels in every pitch game, and whether the default is a toggle or one fixed choice. Picking the wrong default confuses Spanish and European players.
- Queries: `fixed movable do sight singing children study`; `lenguaje musical solfeo do fijo`; `enseñanza musical primaria solfeo nombres notas`
- Sources: the empirical study, Spanish conservatorio/LOMLOE curriculum documents, Spanish music-education articles.
- Extract: https://www.academia.edu/69165660/Effect_of_fixed_do_and_movable_do_solfege_instruction_on_the_development_of_sight_singing_skills_in_7_and_8_year_old_children (if paywalled, search for the same title elsewhere), plus 1 Spanish source.
- Budget: 2 searches, 2 extracts.

### 3. Which misconceptions do children have about notation and rhythm, and do colour and shape aids help or hurt?
- Why it matters for the games: visual encoding for every game (colour-coded notes as in Beat Tiles and Brick Beats, size for loudness, height for pitch), and whether colour crutches block reading later (05 raised this as opinion).
- Queries: `children misconceptions beat rhythm notation`; `colour coded notation children reading study`; `high low pitch loud confusion children`
- Sources: music-education journals (JRME, IJME, Psychology of Music), reviews. Avoid teacher blogs unless they cite research.
- Budget: 2 searches, 2 extracts.

### 4. Is there evidence that sound-before-symbol or audiation-first works better? (Gordon's MLT)
- Why it matters for the games: it justifies the "play it, play with it, hear it, see it written" unlock loop. We also need to know how strongly to hold that loop when a child wants to read early.
- Queries: `audiation before notation study children`; `rote before note reading outcomes`
- Sources: GIML (Gordon Institute for Music Learning) materials and empirical studies.
- Extract: https://thewayschildrenlearnmusic.wordpress.com/2018/07/19/music-language-analogies-sound-before-sight-before-theory (only for its cited studies; follow 1 of them if one is cited).
- Budget: 1 search, 1–2 extracts.

### 5. How are scales, keys, major/minor and simple left-hand accompaniment taught to children aged 6–10 at the piano?
- Why it matters for the games: harmony and chord games on a real piano (Piano Jump), "home note" and key-feel tasks, and the order of the I–IV–V triads.
- Queries: `teaching major minor children piano`; `beginner piano chords accompaniment children sequence`
- Sources: piano pedagogy method publishers (describe ideas only), Orff harmony materials, pedagogy texts.
- Extract: https://www.musicedconsultants.net/uploads/1/0/1/6/101642100/dps_elem_music_workshop.pdf (bordun to chord sequence), plus 1 piano-pedagogy source.
- Budget: 1 search, 2 extracts.

### 6. (Optional, use the spare search) How do Dalcroze and Suzuki turn practice into play and movement at ages 4–7?
- Why it matters for the games: movement-to-music mappings (walk = quarter, run = eighths) for body or camera games, and Suzuki's listening-first repetition.
- Query: `Dalcroze eurhythmics walk run rhythm children`
- Sources: Dalcroze Society or Suzuki Association pages.
- Budget: 1 search, 0–1 extract. Skip it if Q1–Q5 use up the budget.

## Verify (against a primary source)
1. The pentatonic-first rationale (half steps and wide range were too hard, so so-mi became the entry interval). Check it against OAKE, Kodály Institute or a Kodály text, not Wikipedia. This can be folded into the Q1 extracts.
2. The singing range for under-5s (start D4–A4, avoid middle C), as claimed by ChoralNet. Find the underlying study, or confirm it in Music Together's research PDF (https://www.musictogether.com/content/media-files/MTasaResearch-basedProgram.pdf, a vendor source, so mark it as vendor) or in a choral pedagogy text.
3. The claim that steady beat takes about a year at age 5–6 before rhythm is taught (vendor blog). Cross-check against the Kodály sequence from Q1.
4. The origin and design of the Gordon du/de rhythm syllables (the blog says they were adapted in 1993). Check against GIML only if a Q4 source covers it; otherwise drop this check.

## Drop / out of scope
- History and biography (Bartók and Brahms anecdotes, Curwen and Glover origins). They add nothing to game design.
- Broad "which method is best" reviews like the IJRP abstract. The blend-the-methods conclusion is already enough.
- Yamaha and other franchise programmes. They are vendor material and duplicate what Kodály and Orff already give us.
- Takadimi, and anything above ages 10–11 (for example college sight-singing).
- Copyrighted song or exercise content from method books. Describe the sequence only.
