# 03 Engagement and gamification: what makes games engaging

Scope: general game design and motivation psychology, especially for children; when gamification helps learning and when it backfires.

## Subquestions
- Q1 Motivation theory: Self-Determination Theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness), flow, intrinsic vs extrinsic, overjustification effect in children.
- Q2 Game design frameworks: MDA, core loops, "juice"/game feel, short-term vs long-term goals, mastery curves, difficulty adaptation (dynamic difficulty).
- Q3 Gamification evidence in education: points/badges/leaderboards, streaks — what works for 4–10, what harms.
- Q4 Play types for young children: pretend play, exploration/sandbox, collection, narrative, toy-like vs game-like design (e.g. Toca Boca philosophy).
- Q5 Failure, frustration and feedback for young kids: forgiveness, retries, praise of effort, avoiding punishment loops.
- Q6 Session length, attention spans, and designing for parent-child co-play.
- Q7 Ethical design for kids: no dark patterns, no compulsion loops.
