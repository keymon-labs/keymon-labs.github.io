# 03 Engagement — P2 plan

Budget for P2 gatherer: 10 searches, 10 extracts. Planned use: 8 searches + 8 extracts for the questions below, 2 + 2 for Verify. Stop at the cap and list what is left under "Gaps".

Lens for every card: does this mechanic make the child do *more correct practice*, or does it replace practice with something else? Record both kinds of finding.

## 1. What we know (judged)

- **Solid:** Expected, task-contingent tangible rewards lower later free-choice interest; informational feedback does not. Backed by an SDT-author review (Ryan et al. 2019). The d values (-0.40, +0.31) come only from a vendor blog, so verify them (V1). Evidence in under-6s is mixed (Feingold & Mahoney 1975). That is the gap that matters most for a 4–10 band.
- **Solid:** Manipulative design is common in apps for 3–5s, about 80% (Radesky 2022, JAMA Netw Open, primary). It gives us a ready-made anti-pattern checklist.
- **Solid but off-target:** Gamification raises learning outcomes on average (g 0.82, primary-school users larger; Frontiers 2023 meta-analysis). The studies are heterogeneous, likely subject to publication bias, and run in classrooms rather than in home music practice.
- **Moderate:** Points, badges, leaderboards and competition are the elements most often linked to harm, including gaming the system (2022 mapping). It counts reports, not rates, and mostly covers older learners.
- **Moderate, small sample:** Streaks and loss framing cause low-effort "keep the streak" play and guilt in 8–11s (IDC 2026, qualitative). Children younger than 8 are not covered.
- **Moderate, wrong ages:** Process praise beats ability praise (Mueller & Dweck; a 2018 fifth-grade study). It is not shown for 4–7s, and the 67%/92% figures are unverified (V2). Later large mindset replications are weaker, and this was not checked.
- **Weak (practitioner or snippet):** Toca Boca's "toys, not games" stance, co-play design (a Cooney Center blog summary), and the claim that DDA helps (adult players, abstracts only).
- **Missing:** No P1 source ties any engagement mechanic to *music practice* or to keeping *quality* up rather than volume. MDA, juice and flow have no sources yet. That is the biggest hole for game design.

## 2. Phase 2 questions (ranked)

### P2-Q1 Do game rewards in music practice raise correct practice, or just time-on-app?
- Why: This is the project's core risk, engagement replacing practice. We need any evidence (studies, teacher reports) on practice apps, sticker charts and gamified practice trackers for children, and on the effect on quality and persistence.
- Queries: `gamified music practice children study`, `practice reward chart piano motivation`, `music practice app children motivation`
- Sources: music-education journals (JRME, IJME, Psychology of Music), teacher-practitioner articles, studies of apps.
- Extract: nothing yet in Leads, so pick the best 2 hits. Prefer a study over a vendor.

### P2-Q2 Feedback on errors for ages 4–7: what keeps a young child retrying, and what makes them quit?
- Why: Every wrong note or late tap triggers feedback, and P1 praise evidence covers only ages 10–13. We need young-child evidence on error signals (sound, visuals, "try again"), retry design, and praise wording.
- Queries: `young children feedback errors games`, `preschool persistence praise study`, `learning games failure feedback children`
- Sources: developmental psychology (e.g. Cimpian/Gunderson process-praise work with ages 4–5 and toddlers), IDC/CHI papers on children's learning games.
- Extract: none in Leads. Prefer a primary study with 4–7s.

### P2-Q3 Juice and game feel: do they help learning or distract young children?
- Why: Juice is the cheapest engagement tool we have. If it draws attention away from the musical signal (pitch, timing), it works against the goal. We also need what "good juice" looks like.
- Queries: `juice it or lose it`, `seductive details children learning games`, `game feel feedback learning`
- Sources: the GDC talk (Jonasson & Purho), Swink's Game Feel, seductive-details or extraneous-load research (Mayer), HCI studies that test juicy against plain versions.
- Extract: the GDC talk page or a transcript summary, plus one empirical study on juice or seductive details in learning.

### P2-Q4 Session structure for 4–10: short sessions, natural stopping points, coming back the next day without streaks
- Why: Real practice needs regular short sessions. We need a non-coercive return mechanic (the alternative to streaks) and session-length norms for game play (02-technique covers the practice norms).
- Queries: `children app session length attention`, `natural stopping points children apps`, `joint media engagement design guidelines`
- Sources: ACM IDC/CHI, Ganz Cooney Center, AAP guidance.
- Extract: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3816918 (parent-child JME design guidelines), plus one source on session length or stopping points.

### P2-Q5 Toy versus game for music: when should goals and scoring appear, and do 4–6s and 7–10s differ?
- Why: This decides whether each game opens with a sandbox or a level. We need age-specific play-type evidence and Toca's player types (the story lover, creator and master named in the talk).
- Queries: `Toca Boca play design talk`, `open-ended versus goal play children learning`, `age differences children game preferences`
- Sources: practitioner talks (Toca Boca), developmental play research, the Cooney Center.
- Extract: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QUdG7AIJ1s (transcript or summary), plus one developmental source.

### P2-Q6 K-12 gamification by element and by age: which elements help motivation in younger children?
- Why: This turns "avoid points and badges" into a ranked list for our age band.
- Queries: use 1 search only if the Leads fail: `gamification meta-analysis K-12 motivation`
- Sources: meta-analyses.
- Extract: https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/7/3550 (effects by element, K-12). Extract https://www.ovid.com/journals/psys/fulltext/10.1002/pits.70056~a-metaanalysis-of-gamifications-impact-on-student-motivation only if it is reachable without a paywall.

### P2-Q7 (only if budget remains) MDA and flow in children: a usable framing for the design docs
- Why: It is nice to have. A shared vocabulary for P4 game seeds; it does not decide anything by itself.
- Queries: `MDA framework Hunicke`, `flow children learning games`
- Extract: the MDA paper (find the canonical PDF URL by search).

## 3. Verify (primary sources)

- V1: Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (Psychological Bulletin). Confirm the free-choice d values: -0.40 for expected tangible, engagement-contingent rewards and +0.31 for verbal rewards. Check whether there is a moderator for children against college students. Search: `Deci Koestner Ryan 1999 meta-analysis`.
- V2: Mueller & Dweck 1998 (JPSP). Confirm the share of children choosing the challenging task by praise type (the reported 67% vs 92%), and confirm participant ages.
- V3: The IDC 2026 streaks paper. Confirm sample size, ages and app type. The DOI is already in the notes (https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3773077.3806127). Do this only if V1 or V2 is cheap.
- V4: Evans 2015 claim, held by 02-technique, that "no child whose parents used rewards continued past a year". This is a strong overjustification claim specific to music. Hand it to 02 if it is not checked here, and flag it in cross either way.

## 4. Drop / out of scope

- General adult gamification (Duolingo leagues and similar) beyond what P1 already holds; it adds nothing new.
- Complex DDA methods (emotion-based, ML). Simple tempo and window steps are enough; the parameters belong to 04 and 05.
- Monetisation, retention metrics and live-ops design. Our apps have no ads and no accounts.
- Regulation detail (UK AADC, DSA). One line in the ethics checklist is enough.
- Arguments over growth-mindset interventions in schools, beyond V2.

## 5. Hand-offs

- **02-technique:** Practice-session length norms by age (P1 already has 5–20 min) and the Evans 2015 rewards claim (V4). We use their numbers rather than searching again.
- **04-coordination-ux:** Timing-window sizes, adaptive tempo values, and target and touch sizes. We only decide *that* we adapt and how it is framed to the child.
- **05-rhythm-games:** Postmortems on forgiving timing and "performing versus grading" (PaRappa, NecroDancer, Rhythm Heaven). That is our Q5 failure question applied to rhythm; we cite them, not re-research them.

- **06-fourth-wall:** Using physical-world prompts (clap, hold up the mat) as engagement beats. Ethics overlap: the reach-out must not become parasocial pressure, so apply our Radesky checklist.
