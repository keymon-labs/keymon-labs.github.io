# 04 Coordination and UX of interactive games for kids

Scope: motor and timing development, and UX design for interactive (especially rhythm/coordination) games for 4–10.

## Subquestions
- Q1 Motor development 4–10: fine motor, finger independence, bimanual coordination, hand-eye; what is realistic at each age.
- Q2 Rhythm and timing development: beat synchronisation/entrainment by age, tapping accuracy, tempo ranges kids sync best with.
- Q3 Timing windows and latency in rhythm games: judgement windows, audio/video/input latency calibration, how forgiving to be for kids.
- Q4 Feedback design: visual/audio/haptic cues, anticipation cues (note highways, approach circles), error feedback that teaches.
- Q5 UX for young children: touch targets, non-readers (icons, voice), navigation, onboarding through play, guidance from adults.
- Q6 Games known to train coordination (e.g. clapping games, Simon, Dance Dance Revolution, exergames, movement games) and evidence.
- Q7 Accessibility and different abilities (left-handed, small hands, attention differences).
