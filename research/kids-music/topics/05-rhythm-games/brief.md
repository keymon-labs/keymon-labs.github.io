# 05 Music and rhythm games: how they worked and why they engaged

Scope: commercial and educational music/rhythm games, their mechanics, and evidence of learning transfer.

## Subquestions
- Q1 History and mechanics: PaRappa the Rapper, Beatmania, DDR, Taiko no Tatsujin, Guitar Hero/Rock Band, Rhythm Heaven, Patapon, Elite Beat Agents, Crypt of the NecroDancer, Beat Saber, Thumper, Just Dance — what each did and why it was engaging (designer interviews/postmortems).
- Q2 Rock Band Pro mode / Rocksmith / real-instrument games: did they teach real skills?
- Q3 Learning apps: Simply Piano, Flowkey, Yousician, Piano Maestro, Synthesia, Melodics, Chrome Music Lab, Toca Band, Rhythm Cat, Note Rush — mechanics, strengths, criticisms (e.g. "follow the falling notes" vs reading).
- Q4 Evidence: do rhythm/music games transfer to real musicianship? Studies on music apps for children.
- Q5 Design patterns: note highway vs staff, call-and-response, rhythm "remixes", rhythm Heaven-style audio-only cues, story/character wrapping.
- Q6 Music toys and creative tools for kids (sequencers, loopers, tangible music toys) and what keeps kids returning.
